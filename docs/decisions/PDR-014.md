# PDR-014: Narrow `action_items` to the writer's own direct actions — remove third-party-attributed commitments

**Date**: 2026-09-11
**Status**: Accepted

## Decision

Reverses the "`action_items` ownership" convention settled 2026-08-23 during the third adversarial re-review (`REVIEW_GUIDE.md` §4, `DATASET_SPEC.md`'s "Ownership" rule). That convention held that any named person's forward commitment belonged in `action_items`, attributed to them (`"Uncle Bob to handle the catering"`).

New rule: `action_items` holds only tasks the **writer themself** has a direct action to take. A third party's stated commitment, expected arrival, or intention — however real, however worth tracking — is not an action item for the writer, because the writer has nothing to *do* about it. This content is not dropped from the record: it still belongs in `narrative` and `bullets`, exactly as stated, with its hedge intact. It is removed from `action_items` specifically, because that field's job is the writer's own to-do list, not a record of everyone's stated plans.

**Explicitly not permitted as a substitute**: no invented tracking/follow-up action item takes the vacated slot (e.g. `"Confirm Sam is driving"` when `input` never stated a need to confirm anything). That would violate this project's own "no invented content" evidence-first rule just as much as the thing being fixed — the corpus doesn't get to lose a real defect and gain an invented one in the same edit.

**Two edge cases, resolved directly (round-1 review-bridge finding, not in the original scan):**
- **Group imperatives ("we need to X") stay in `action_items`.** "We" includes the writer directly — this is the writer's own action, jointly held, not a third party's.
- **Unassigned imperatives ("someone needs to X") are removed, same as any other non-writer attribution.** Naming no one, including not naming the writer, means the writer has no more claim on it than "Sam" or "Uncle Bob" do — it stays in narrative/bullets like any other third-party-shaped commitment.

**A third case, caught by the review bridge (round-2 finding) and confirmed correct: writer imperatives that name a third party as their object stay in `action_items`.** "Tell Sam to drive," "Ask Uncle Bob about catering," "Remind Chloe about the citations" — the writer holds the action verb (tell, ask, remind); the third party is who the writer's own action is directed at, not who is doing the committing. This is a different shape from `"Sam to drive"` or `"Uncle Bob to handle the catering"`, where the third party holds the verb. The distinguishing test stays the same in both cases — does the *writer* have something to do — these two shapes just answer it oppositely. Checked directly against the 35-entry scope list below: none of the entries found are this shape (all are `"[Name] to/is/will [verb]"`, the third party holding the verb), so this finding changes the rule text but not the scope list.

## Reasoning

### Why re-open a settled convention, not just patch two records

The immediate trigger was reading the held-section experiment's re-run output on Qwen3.5-4B (2026-09-11): record #6 (`Sam will drive us to the airport. I'm just so cranky today. I need to book the hotel.`) omitted `"Sam to drive to the airport"` from `action_items`. Checked against the existing rule, that omission looked like a model defect — the existing convention explicitly calls for it. On inspection, the existing convention is the actual defect: it asks `action_items` to hold a task nobody in the conversation, including the writer, is being asked to complete. Sam's commitment is real information; it just isn't the writer's action.

The product owner's own framing: rules written under old circumstances — an earlier model base, an earlier stage of the project's own understanding — aren't permanently binding just because they were once deliberately settled. This project has already revisited a settled boundary once before, for a different reason and in a different direction (PDR-013's narrow content-safety carve-out) — the same willingness to re-examine a rule against what the project has since learned applies here too, in the opposite direction (narrowing rather than carving an exception into a boundary).

### Why the old rule's own worked example doesn't survive scrutiny

The rule's anchor example, `"Uncle Bob to handle the catering"`, was treated as obviously a valid action item. Re-examined directly: Uncle Bob handling the catering is something the writer would put in their narrative and bullets without hesitation — it's real, relevant planning information. But it is not something the *writer* does. Filing it under `action_items` implicitly asks the writer to act on a task that isn't theirs, which is a category error the old rule's own stated purpose ("a note-organizer that silently drops other people's commitments loses real planning information") doesn't actually require — narrative and bullets already preserve that information without needing `action_items` to also carry it.

### Scope, measured directly rather than guessed

An initial corpus scan found ~35 entries across ~20 records, but that scan's own pattern was too narrow — it missed multi-word names ("Uncle Bob," "Cousin Sarah"), reported-speech commitments ("Sarah said she would..."), modal-led commitments ("Cousin Sarah might also try..."), colon-attributed tasks ("David: Pack the garage..."), and other reported-commitment verbs ("Dave promised to sweep"). Three further passes, each checking the prior pass's blind spot directly against the corpus rather than assuming the first pass was exhaustive, found the true scope: **60 entries across 37 records**, concentrated in `multi_person_note` as expected but also present in `time_ambiguous`, `self_correction`, `rapid_branching`, `dangling_reference`, and `repeated_reminder`. A final exhaustive read of every remaining `action_items` entry corpus-wide (532 records) confirmed no further instances.

`multi_person_note`'s own `TAXONOMY.md` definition ("correctly attribute a fragment to the right person... rather than merging or reassigning") is about narrative/bullets-level attribution, not specifically about what belongs in `action_items` — this change doesn't undercut the category's actual teaching lesson, only removes one field's participation in it.

## Scope

**Requires updates to:**
- `docs/datasets/REVIEW_GUIDE.md` §4's "`action_items` ownership" bullet and §6b's cross-reference to it
- `training/DATASET_SPEC.md`'s "Ownership" rule under `action_items`
- `datasets/synthetic.jsonl` — every third-party-attributed `action_items` entry found in the scan above, removed from that field only (content stays in narrative/bullets, unchanged)
- Standard post-fix checks: schema validation, duplicate check, copy-ratio check, full three-check voice-regression suite — same as every other corpus-wide fix this project has done

**Not in scope:**
- `real_validation.jsonl` / `real_holdout.jsonl` — hand-written by the product owner, not Claude's to touch, per standing rule; if any of his own real notes use the old convention, that's his call whether to revise, not an automatic corpus-wide sweep
- `training/experiments/held_section/raw_batch.jsonl` — the held-section experiment's own hand-built batch was generated under the old convention too (record #6 among others); left untouched for now since that experiment is still unpromoted and pre-dates this decision either way

## Executed

60 entries removed from `action_items` across 37 records in `datasets/synthetic.jsonl` (content preserved in narrative/bullets throughout — nothing dropped from the record, only moved out of that one field). The two mechanical risks flagged during review were checked per-record as committed, not deferred:

- **`zero_action_items`-shaped drift**: exactly 2 records (both `hard`-tier — `contradictory_statement` and `multi_person_note`) had their `action_items` emptied by this sweep. Both read individually: in each, the writer never stated a commitment of their own — every candidate action belonged to a third party (Ben's terrarium purchase; Mark's/Chloe's prop opinions) — so an empty `action_items` is the correct outcome, not a side effect needing separate review. The other 42 non-`zero_action_items`-category records already carrying empty `action_items` corpus-wide predate this sweep entirely and are unrelated.
- **`expert`-tag erosion**: 15 touched records are `expert`-tier. Checked against `TAXONOMY.md`'s own definition ("dense combination of categories... more restated/branching content" — a narrative/bullets-level judgment, confirmed by this project's own standing "structural ceiling" principle). Since narrative/bullets are untouched by this sweep, none of the 15 lose their structural basis for the tag. No downgrades applied.

Standard post-fix checks after the sweep: schema validation 532/532 OK, `check_duplicates.py`/`check_copy_ratio.py` show no new findings (same pre-existing allowlisted items as before this session), full `pytest tests/` 31/31 passed.
