# PDR-015: Full removal of self-harm/violence content from all output fields; retire the `held` experiment

**Date**: 2026-09-12
**Status**: Accepted

## Decision

Supersedes [PDR-013](PDR-013.md)'s narrative-retention clause. PDR-013's
constraints 1, 3, 4, and 5 (hand-authored-only by the product owner; a new
dedicated `category` tag; its own dedicated file,
`datasets/self_harm_context_boundary.jsonl`; deliberately small) carry
forward unchanged. Constraint 2 and the narrative-retention half of the
Decision section are replaced by the following.

### 1. Self-harm/violence content is fully excluded from every output field, always

`narrative`, `bullets`, and `action_items` must never contain self-harm or
violence content — ideation, distress statements naming it, or method —
in any form, regardless of how `input` phrases it. This replaces PDR-013's
"`narrative` may retain the content, in sanitized/non-graphic form" with a
stricter rule: no output field retains it at all.

`input` is unaffected by this PDR — it keeps the real content verbatim,
as PDR-013 already established, since it represents what a person
actually typed and the model's recovery task starts from there.

**An action item that is the writer's own genuine, separable task may
still appear in `action_items`**, even when what prompted it was
self-harm/violence-adjacent distress — e.g. input naming both a dark
thought and "I need to call the doctor to adjust my medication" still
produces that action item. The task is not the same content as the
ideation that motivated it; omitting a real, separable, writer-stated task
would violate this project's own "no invented omission" principle as much
as inventing one would. **Any such surviving action item remains governed
by the same ideation-vs-method test as everything else** — a supportive
action naming no method (e.g. "call 988," "talk to a doctor," "call the
doctor to adjust medication") is fine as stated; an action item that
itself names a method or method-adjacent object (e.g. disposing of a
specific means) fails this test and is dropped like anywhere else. This
was added during review-bridge round 1 on this PDR (see "Approved by"):
the original draft's separable-action exception risked reading as license
to invent a new, narrower carve-out for crisis-specific vocabulary; tying
it explicitly to the existing test closes that gap without inventing a
second rule or requiring generic-vocabulary substitution for content that
was never actually excluded (a hotline number or "talk to a doctor" names
no method and needs no sanitizing).

Method-specificity remains permanently banned in every field, unchanged
from PDR-013's own "ideation-vs-method" test (added 2026-09-12): naming a
feeling/wish with no means attached was already the only eligible shape,
and remains so — this PDR does not relax that test, it removes the
narrative-only retention that test was gating.

### 2. The `held` experiment is retired

`training/experiments/held_section/` (added 2026-09-09, per PDR-012) and
the 4th "held" output field it tested are retired outright — changed from
PDR-012's "still a separate, experimental, undecided feature" to formally
closed, not a live follow-up. See
[`training/experiments/held_section/RETIRED.md`](../../training/experiments/held_section/RETIRED.md).

Reason: `held`'s mechanism extracts any emotionally intense fragment out
of `narrative` based on affect alone ("content with real affect but no
underlying fact/decision"), independent of what that content actually is.
That is the wrong axis under the principle stated above — emotional
content, of any valence or intensity, belongs in `narrative` exactly as
the writer expressed it; the only legitimate grounds for excluding content
from an output field is content-type (self-harm/violence), never
intensity of feeling.

No corpus content is affected by this retirement — `held` was never
merged into `datasets/synthetic.jsonl` or the production schema
(`training/prepare_data.py`'s `serialize_target`/`deserialize_target`);
only the experimental fork and its "undecided" status in `PDR-012.md` are
closed out.

## Reasoning

### Why intensity was the wrong axis

The product owner's own framing: real, legitimate future applications of
this model diverge in exactly the way `held` tried to prejudge. A
journaling app aimed at emotional processing might reasonably want to
foreground positive emotion and soften negative; a pure task-extraction
app might reasonably want all emotional content stripped regardless of
valence, keeping only actionable content. Both are legitimate products of
"intent recovery," and neither is this project's call to make on behalf of
the other — this model's job is maximal fidelity to what was actually
said, full stop, so that either downstream use case has an equally
faithful base to build its own presentation layer on. `held`'s "pull out
anything that's emotionally intense" was, in effect, making the first
app's choice by default for every downstream consumer, without ever having
been decided as policy.

### Why full removal is the right target for self-harm/violence specifically, not a stricter version of the same idea

Two independent points, not one:

1. **It's a stronger safety property under open-weights**, the same
   argument PDR-013 already made for why training (not a wrapper) is the
   only available mechanism: content that is never present in any output
   field cannot leak through a failure of narrative-only sanitization, and
   there's no field left where a drift could surface it.
2. **It's a simpler, more learnable rule.** PDR-013's "retain in one field,
   exclude from two others, sanitize if needed, never drift into
   method-specificity" is a multi-part conditional. Found empirically,
   2026-09-12: even a careful human author drifted on part of it (4 of 7
   examples named a method despite the ideation-only intent) on a first
   pass. A single binary rule — self-harm/violence content is absent from
   every output field — is a plainer target for both a human author and a
   trained model to hit consistently.

### Why this simplifies existing documentation rather than just changing it

PDR-013's constraint 6 required an explicit carve-out from the cross-field
consistency check in both `DATASET_SPEC.md` and `REVIEW_GUIDE.md` §4,
specifically because `narrative` was deliberately allowed to carry content
`bullets`/`action_items` omitted. Under this PDR, all three fields agree
again (all fully exclude the content) — **that carve-out is removed
entirely**, restoring the general consistency rule with no exception, in
both documents. This is a net simplification, not just a substitution of
one exception for another.

### Relationship to `GOLD_PHILOSOPHY.md`'s "Human-Centered Intent Recovery" principle

PDR-013 blurred two separate things into one exception: the general
principle that emotional/cognitive state is fair game everywhere in this
corpus ("rushed, distracted, excited"), and the specific, narrow
self-harm/violence content-type boundary from PDR-001/PDR-005. This PDR
restores the original clean separation: the "emotion is context" principle
is reaffirmed and generalized — it was never meant to have a
higher-intensity carve-out in the first place — while self-harm/violence
remains purely a PDR-005-style content-type boundary, unconnected to how
emotionally intense the surrounding note is.

## Scope

**Executed as part of this acceptance:**
- `docs/vision/GOLD_PHILOSOPHY.md` — eighth principle re-amended to
  reflect full removal, not retention, referencing this PDR
- `training/DATASET_SPEC.md` — hard content boundary section restated for
  full exclusion
- `docs/datasets/TAXONOMY.md` — `self_harm_context_boundary` entry
  rewritten: the lesson is now "recognize and fully exclude, while
  preserving any genuinely separable writer-stated action," not "retain in
  narrative only"; no longer described as exempt from §0/§4
- `docs/datasets/REVIEW_GUIDE.md` — §0's exception paragraph rewritten
  (no narrative exception remains — full exclusion checked like any other
  content in this file, plus the action-item method-specificity test
  spelled out); §4's PDR-013 carve-out **removed**, not replaced —
  restores the general "fields must agree" rule with no exception
- `docs/decisions/PDR-012.md` — "Explicitly out of scope" `held` line
  updated to point at this PDR's retirement rather than "still undecided"
- `training/experiments/held_section/RETIRED.md` — added; code left in
  place as historical record per this project's convention, but no longer
  referenced anywhere as an open follow-up
- `datasets/self_harm_context_boundary.jsonl` — all 7 existing records'
  `narrative` fields re-edited to remove the self-harm/violence sentence
  entirely (previously retained, sanitized, per the superseded rule);
  mechanical edit, ordinary emotional content and every other fragment in
  each record's narrative preserved unchanged; re-validated against
  `training_data.schema.json` and `prepare_data.load_jsonl()` (7/7 OK),
  re-run against `check_duplicates.py` across the full corpus (no new
  flags), full `pytest tests/` (31/31 passed)

**Known, separate, pre-existing gap — not fixed by this PDR:**
`training/generate_blind_rereview_prompt.py`'s embedded `CHECKLIST_TEXT`
(a hardcoded copy of the review checklist used to generate Gemini's blind
re-review prompts) was never updated for PDR-013's carve-out in the first
place — a gap in PDR-013's own Scope section, not created by this PDR.
Under this PDR the "fields must agree" half of that gap resolves itself
(no carve-out needed anymore, so the checklist's un-amended general rule
is now correct again), but the hard-gate half does not: that checklist
would still flag any record as an immediate REJECT purely for `input`
containing a self-harm mention, which is legitimate and by-design in this
one file. Not fixed here because this script defaults to
`datasets/synthetic.jsonl` and may never actually be pointed at
`self_harm_context_boundary.jsonl` — flagged for the product owner's own
future decision, not silently dropped.

**Not retroactive beyond the above:**
- `datasets/synthetic.jsonl`'s existing 532 records are unaffected — built
  entirely under the permanent depiction/instruction/normalization ban
  (PDR-005), never containing this content in any form regardless of this
  PDR.

**Does not, by itself, trigger a retrain.** Same standing note as
PDR-013 — the edited examples and incorporating them into a future
`prepare_data.py`/`train.py` run are separate, later decisions.

## Approved by

- Product Owner — directed this PDR be drafted, 2026-09-12, generalizing
  PDR-013's exception after finding it hard to apply consistently even by
  hand, and after concluding the project's actual design goal was never to
  hide heavy emotion, which `held`'s premise had drifted toward without
  ever being decided as policy. Accepted the draft without a second
  review-bridge round, 2026-09-12.
- Engineering Lead (Claude) — drafted this PDR; executed the Scope changes
  above and verified them directly (schema validation, duplicate check,
  full test suite) rather than assuming they held.
- Gemini review-bridge, 1 round (manual transport, no API credits
  available 2026-09-12), verdict Approve with changes, ALIGNED. Two
  findings: (1) the separable-action-item exception needed an explicit
  boundary for crisis-specific action items — accepted, and resolved by
  tying it to the existing ideation-vs-method test rather than Gemini's
  own suggested remedy (forcing crisis vocabulary into generic
  substitutes), which was checked and found broader than PDR-005 actually
  requires, since a supportive action naming no method isn't self-harm
  content in the first place. (2) the Scope list should include automated
  dataset-linting/CI enforcement of cross-field consistency — checked
  directly against this repository: the specific files Gemini named
  (`scripts/validate_dataset.py`, a CI workflow) don't exist here, but a
  real analogous gap does (`generate_blind_rereview_prompt.py`'s embedded
  checklist, see above) — recorded as a known, separate, pre-existing
  issue rather than folded into this PDR's Scope, since it isn't this
  PDR's Scope that created or worsens it.
