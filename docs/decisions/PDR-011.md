# PDR-011: Recalibrate "recovery" to the product owner's actual writing register, not the synthetic corpus's internally-derived convention

**Date**: 2026-09-08
**Status**: Accepted

## Decision

Three of this project's core output conventions change, based on direct
comparison against all 15 `real_validation.jsonl` records (the product
owner's own hand-written notes — never drafted by any AI, per the
project's standing rule):

1. **Narrative reorganization target relaxes.** The synthetic corpus's
   established input→narrative similarity target (mean 0.561, 0.85 treated
   as an automatic defect line) is recalibrated toward the real notes'
   actual mean (0.690). 0.85 is no longer an automatic defect — read
   deliberately, not auto-flagged, closer to how the real corpus already
   sits (1 of 15 real records is already above it).
2. **Bullets switch from first-person imperative to third-person,
   descriptive voice.** Reverses this project's own first-person-voice
   convention (established after the 6th periodic re-review found 26
   third-person narratives/bullets and rewrote them to first person) —
   for bullets specifically, not narrative. Target register: "Needs to
   buy milk," "Expressed desire to start working out again" — matching
   what 56 of 60 (93%) of the product owner's own real bullets already do.
   `narrative` stays first-person; this only changes `bullets`.
3. **A stated need or desire becomes an `action_items` entry, cleaned up
   into an imperative** — "need to get back to working out" →
   `action_items: ["Begin workouts again"]` — rather than staying
   empty for lacking a hard, dated commitment. Refines, not reverses,
   the 13th re-review's "imperative mood alone doesn't make something an
   action item" rule: a self-suggested, open-ended possible activity
   ("watch movies, play with the dog," nothing decided) still stays out
   of `action_items`; a stated need ("need to get back to working out") is
   a real if modest self-directed commitment and now belongs in it,
   rephrased as a clean command.

## Reasoning

### What was checked directly, not estimated

External review (Claude API/Fable 5.1, 2026-09-07, finding C3) flagged
this by hand-estimating 3 of 15 real records. Before deciding anything,
recomputed both claims against the full 15:

- **Copy ratio**: `word_ratio(narrative, input)` per real record — mean
  **0.690** (range 0.429–0.889), confirming Fable's estimate and extending
  it to the full set. 1 of 15 (#9, 0.889) already sits above the corpus's
  own 0.85 defect line.
- **Bullet register**: 56 of 60 real bullets (93%) are third-person or
  subject-elided ("Needs to...", "Expresses...", "Thinks...") — a stronger
  and more uniform result than Fable's 4 quoted examples suggested.
- **One correction to Fable's claim**: Fable said real bullets "often"
  carry terminal periods. Checked all 60 — only 2 of 60 do. The corpus's
  existing "no terminal period" rule (re-enforced this same session,
  PDR-level fix in the C4 disposition) was already correctly calibrated;
  it's specifically the first-person-vs-third-person *voice* that
  diverged, not punctuation.

### Why the product owner's register wins, not the synthetic corpus's

Put directly to the product owner rather than decided unilaterally: "Cleaner
output does not mean losing key information... I think we would want to
reorganize less aggressively." The synthetic corpus's conventions
(first-person bullets, tighter reorganization, a hard 0.85 gate) were
derived entirely from within the synthetic-generation loop and checked
against `REVIEW_GUIDE.md`'s own internal consistency — never against the
15 real records, which the project's docs state were deliberately kept
out of that loop specifically so they could serve as an untouched ground
truth. Once actually compared, the two diverge on exactly the two axes
this project has policed hardest. The real notes define what this tool is
actually supposed to produce; the synthetic conventions were a proxy for
that; the proxy needs to match the target, not the reverse.

### Why this doesn't reopen "no invented certainty" or "no dropped
imperatives"

A stated need ("need to get back to working out") is a real, if modest,
self-directed obligation — distinct in kind from a genuine hedge/musing
("maybe I'll..."), which stays hedged, and distinct from the 13th
re-review's "self-suggested, open-ended possibility" carve-out ("watch
movies, play with the dog" — nothing determined, no external party to
disappoint), which stays empty. Cleaning "need to X" up into an imperative
is not inventing a commitment that wasn't there; the need itself is the
commitment, just phrased the way the person actually wrote it rather than
as a polished command. Both existing rules stay as they are for the cases
they were built for.

## Existing-corpus disposition: Legacy Baseline, no retrofit

This PDR settles what "recovery" means going forward; it does not
retrofit ~530 existing synthetic records whose bullets are in the old
first-person convention, or whose `action_items` were left empty under
the old stated-need rule. That is a substantially larger undertaking than
any single fix this session has made (C1/C2/C4 combined touched 64
records; this potentially touches most of the corpus's bullets field).
**Decided, 2026-09-08**: no retrofit. The existing ~530-record corpus is a
permanent **Legacy Baseline** — old first-person-bullet convention, old
copy-ratio target — left as-is because it is still good and trainable on
its own terms. The new convention (relaxed reorganization target,
third-person bullets, stated-need action items) applies only to
generation from this point forward. This means the corpus is now, and
stays, a deliberately mixed-convention dataset: older records in one
register, newer ones in another, neither retroactively "fixed" to match
the other. Not a defect to reconcile later unless a future training
result specifically suggests the mix itself is a problem.

**Follow-up doc/tooling updates this PDR requires, tracked so they aren't
lost** (also per Gemini's review): `training/DATASET_SPEC.md`'s bullet/
action_items field definitions, `docs/datasets/REVIEW_GUIDE.md`'s §4 (no
invented certainty / no dropped imperatives) and §6b (voice/register
regression checks — the existing first-person-bullet regex would now
incorrectly flag the *new* convention) both need rewriting to describe
this register as correct, and `training/check_copy_ratio.py`'s threshold/
allowlist-ceiling logic needs re-reading against the new ~0.69 target
rather than the old ~0.561 one.

## Scope

- Applies to `bullets` and `action_items` register/content rules and the
  narrative copy-ratio target, corpus-wide, going forward.
- Does not change `narrative`'s own voice (stays first-person) or any of
  the three verbatim-preservation categories' rules
  (`interrupted_thought`/`contradictory_statement`/`dangling_reference`)
  or `self_correction`'s drop-the-retraction rule (`PDR` via this
  session's C2 disposition) — those are independent of register.
- `check_copy_ratio.py`'s allowlist/ceiling mechanism, `REVIEW_GUIDE.md`'s
  §4/§6b checklist text, and `DATASET_SPEC.md`'s field definitions all need
  updating to reflect this — tracked as follow-up work, not done in this
  PDR.
- Revisit if a future batch of real notes (the product owner's nightly
  collection, ongoing per the real-validation-collection plan) shows a
  materially different register than these 15.
