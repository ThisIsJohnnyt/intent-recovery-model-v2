REVIEWER ONBOARDING EXAMPLES — illustrative only, NOT from the corpus
========================================================================

These 4 examples were written from scratch, just now, for the purpose of
building a "how this works" explainer for new human reviewers. They do
NOT appear in datasets/synthetic.jsonl and were never generated by
Gemini or reviewed against the project's own checklist — don't pull them
into the real corpus later by mistake.

Purpose: show a new reviewer, who has never seen this project, what one
"example" actually is (a messy input note + the app's cleaned-up
recovery of it), and the three questions we actually want their
reaction to — not a rules checklist, just a human read.

========================================================================
WHAT THIS APP DOES, IN ONE PARAGRAPH

People jot down notes the way real notes actually look — scattered,
half-finished, sometimes contradicting themselves, sometimes with no
clear next step at all. This app takes that raw note ("input" below) and
tries to recover what the person actually meant: a clean one-paragraph
summary ("narrative"), the key points as a list ("bullets"), and any
concrete to-dos it can find ("action items"). Your job as a reviewer is
NOT to check the app's work against a rulebook — we already do that.
Your job is to read it the way you'd read your own note being handed
back to you, and tell us if it feels right.

For each example, answer in a sentence or two:

  1. Does the INPUT read like something a real person actually typed?
     (Not this app's writing — imagine a friend jotted this on their phone.)
  2. If this were YOUR note, is the NARRATIVE what you'd want handed back?
     Not "is it technically correct" — is it actually useful?
  3. Is there anything here worth flagging — good or bad?

--- 1 of 4 --- [simple_list / easy]

INPUT:
  milk, eggs, the good bread not the cheap kind, coffee filters, dog food

NARRATIVE:
  I need to pick up milk, eggs, the good bread (not the cheap kind),
  coffee filters, and dog food.

BULLETS:
  - Milk
  - Eggs
  - The good bread, not the cheap kind
  - Coffee filters
  - Dog food

ACTIONS:
  - Buy milk, eggs, the good bread, coffee filters, and dog food

  1. input reads real?
  2. narrative useful?
  3. worth learning?

--- 2 of 4 --- [dangling_reference / hard]

INPUT:
  need to call the place back about the thing with my car, they said
  it might need the part replaced but weren't sure, also pick up the
  thing from Dana's before Friday

NARRATIVE:
  I need to call the place back about the issue with my car — they
  said it might need a part replaced, but weren't sure. I also need to
  pick up the thing from Dana's before Friday.

BULLETS:
  - Need to call the shop back about my car
  - They said it might need a part replaced but weren't sure
  - Need to pick up the thing from Dana's before Friday

ACTIONS:
  - Call the place back about the car issue
  - Pick up the thing from Dana's before Friday

  1. input reads real?
  2. narrative useful?
  3. worth learning?

--- 3 of 4 --- [contradictory_statement / medium]

INPUT:
  gonna commit to running every morning this week starting tomorrow.
  actually my knee has been bothering me since Tuesday so maybe I
  should just stick to walking for now

NARRATIVE:
  I was going to commit to running every morning this week starting
  tomorrow, but my knee has been bothering me since Tuesday, so maybe I
  should just stick to walking for now.

BULLETS:
  - Planned to start running every morning this week
  - Knee has been bothering me since Tuesday
  - Maybe should stick to walking for now instead

ACTIONS:
  - Maybe stick to walking for now instead of running

  1. input reads real?
  2. narrative useful?
  3. worth learning?

--- 4 of 4 --- [topic_switching / hard]

INPUT:
  need to email the landlord about the leak under the sink before it
  gets worse. also can't believe how good that new taco place was,
  gonna go back with Sam next week. landlord's number is in the old
  lease paperwork somewhere probably the folder in the office

NARRATIVE:
  I need to email the landlord about the leak under the sink before it
  gets worse — his number is probably in the old lease paperwork,
  likely in the folder in the office. Switching gears, that new taco
  place was so good, I'm planning to go back with Sam next week.

BULLETS:
  - Need to email the landlord about the sink leak before it worsens
  - Landlord's number is probably in the old lease paperwork, maybe the
    office folder
  - The new taco place was really good
  - Want to go back to the taco place with Sam next week

ACTIONS:
  - Email the landlord about the sink leak
  - Go back to the taco place with Sam next week

  1. input reads real?
  2. narrative useful?
  3. worth learning?
